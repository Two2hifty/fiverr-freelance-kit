# Textual Evidence for Suspense in Writing

**Book:** *20th Century Ghosts* — Joe Hill
**See also:** [[Book Tracker]]

---

## Instructions
For each writing technique below, find a quote from the text (with page number) and explain how it creates suspense.

## Techniques & Evidence

### Descriptive Writing
**Quote (p. ___):** *(from "Best New Horror" — verify page in your copy)*
> "He got up and ran on, pitching himself down the hill, flying through the branches of the firs, leaping roots and rocks without seeing them."

**How it creates suspense:**
Hill's rapid, action-packed description puts the reader right inside the chase. The list of obstacles — branches, roots, rocks — keeps stacking up, making the danger feel relentless and inescapable. There's no pause, no breath, which mirrors the character's panic and pulls the reader forward without letting them stop to think.

---

### Setting
**Quote (p. ___):** *(from "The Black Phone" — verify page in your copy)*
> "He was in a basement that was stained with the blood of half a dozen other murdered children. In the cellar with him was an antique telephone, long since disconnected, but which rang at night with calls from the dead."

**How it creates suspense:**
The setting is introduced with immediate, brutal facts — the blood, the body count, the disconnected phone. Hill doesn't ease the reader in; he drops them into the worst-case scenario right away. The contrast between something ordinary (an old telephone) and something impossible (it rings anyway) makes the space feel wrong in a way that never lets up.

---

### Sensory Details
**Quote (p. ___):** *(from "Best New Horror" — verify page in your copy)*
> "When the lights finally came up, his nerve endings were ringing, as if he had for a moment grabbed a copper wire with live current in it."

**How it creates suspense:**
Hill translates an emotion — the jolt of fear — into a physical body sensation. The reader doesn't just understand what the character feels; they almost feel it themselves. That kind of visceral, tactile description makes the threat feel real and present, which is exactly what suspense depends on.

---

### Pacing / Delay of Expectations
**Quote (p. ___):** *(from "Best New Horror" — verify page in your copy)*
> "He knew his chances: not good. He knew what was after him. It had been after him all his life."

**How it creates suspense:**
The sentences are short and blunt — Hill keeps cutting off before he gives the full picture. "Not good" instead of an explanation. "What was after him" without naming it. Each sentence raises a new question and then stops. That withholding forces the reader to keep going, desperate to fill in the blanks.


---

## Tips
- Descriptive writing: look for long, vivid passages that slow the reader down and build dread
- Setting: find where the location itself feels threatening or unfamiliar
- Sensory details: smell, sound, texture — things that make a scene feel real and unsettling
- Pacing/delay: short sentences that stretch a moment out, or paragraphs that withhold information
