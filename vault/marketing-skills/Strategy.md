## Marketing Psychology `/marketing-psychology`

Apply psychological principles and mental models to marketing decisions.

### Foundational thinking models

**First Principles**: Break problems down to basic truths. Ask "why" five times. Don't copy competitors without understanding why something works.

**Jobs to Be Done**: People hire products to do a job. A drill buyer wants a hole. Frame your product around the outcome, not the features.

**Inversion**: Instead of "how do I succeed?" ask "what would guarantee failure?" Then avoid those things systematically.

**Pareto Principle (80/20)**: 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. Find the 20% of channels or customers driving 80% of results. Cut or reduce the rest.

**Occam's Razor**: If conversions dropped, check the obvious first (broken form, page speed) before assuming complex attribution issues.

### Behavioral science principles

**Anchoring**: The first number seen influences all subsequent judgments. Show a higher price first. Present your full price before the discounted price.

**Social Proof**: People follow others' behavior when uncertain. Show customer counts, testimonials, logos, and ratings.

**Loss Aversion**: Losing something feels twice as bad as gaining the same thing feels good. "Don't lose your data" outperforms "keep your data." Frame upgrades as preventing loss.

**Scarcity and Urgency**: Limited availability increases perceived value. Use genuine deadlines and real limits. Fake urgency destroys trust.

**Framing**: The same information framed differently produces different decisions. "95% fat-free" outperforms "5% fat."

**Commitment and Consistency**: Once people take a small action, they are more likely to take larger consistent actions. Low-friction first step (free trial, waitlist) leads to higher conversion.

**Mere Exposure Effect**: People prefer things they've seen before. Repeated brand touchpoints build familiarity and trust, even without conscious recall.

**Availability Heuristic**: People judge probability by how easily examples come to mind. Case studies and specific numbers make benefits feel more real and achievable.

**Confirmation Bias**: People seek information that confirms their existing beliefs. Meet prospects where they already are; don't fight their worldview.

**IKEA Effect**: People value things they helped create. Involve users in setup, customization, or onboarding to increase perceived value and retention.

**Zero-Price Effect**: Free feels disproportionately attractive. A free trial or free tier pulls more sign-ups than a deeply discounted paid option.

**Hyperbolic Discounting**: People over-value immediate rewards and under-value future ones. "Get results today" outperforms "see results in 90 days."

**Paradox of Choice**: Too many options reduce conversion. Fewer, clearer choices outperform feature-rich comparison tables.

**Goal-Gradient Effect**: Effort increases as people get closer to a goal. Show progress indicators in onboarding and subscription flows.

**Peak-End Rule**: People judge experiences by their peak moment and their ending. Make the first win fast and make cancellation a dignified experience.

**Zeigarnik Effect**: People remember incomplete tasks better than completed ones. Onboarding checklists and progress bars exploit this to drive activation.

**Mental Accounting**: People treat money differently based on how they label it. "Costs less than your daily coffee" reframes a $30/month spend.

**Regret Aversion**: People will work harder to avoid regret than to achieve gain. "Don't miss out" framing taps regret aversion more than "get more."

**Bandwagon Effect**: People follow crowds. "Join 50,000 teams" is a conversion lever, not just social proof.

### Application principle
Use psychology to help people make decisions that are good for them. Manipulation (fake urgency, false scarcity) destroys trust and produces short-term gains with long-term damage.

## Launch Strategy `/launch-strategy`

Plan product launches, feature releases, and go-to-market moments.

### Core philosophy
Don't launch once. Launch again and again. Every new feature, improvement, and update is an opportunity to capture attention.

### The ORB Framework

**Owned channels** (build these first):
- Email list
- Blog
- Podcast
- Branded community (Slack, Discord)
- Compound over time. No algorithm dependency.

**Rented channels** (use, but do not depend on):
- Social media platforms
- Marketplaces (Product Hunt, AppSumo)
- Pay-to-play increases over time

**Borrowed channels** (fastest reach, lowest retention):
- Press and media
- Podcast guesting
- Influencer partnerships
- Community posts (Reddit, Hacker News)

### Five-phase launch approach
1. **Internal**: Dog-food the product. Fix critical bugs. Align team on narrative.
2. **Alpha**: Invite 10 to 20 hand-picked users. Get feedback. Refine positioning.
3. **Beta**: Open to 100 to 500 users. Build case studies. Start collecting testimonials.
4. **Early Access**: Waitlist opens. Drive pre-launch demand. Build buzz on rented and borrowed channels.
5. **Full Launch**: Coordinated push across owned, rented, and borrowed channels simultaneously.

### Product Hunt strategy
- Launch on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday
- Go live at 12:01 AM PT
- Have 50+ upvoters ready before noon PT
- Post a genuine comment about the story behind the product
- Respond to every comment within the first 4 hours

## Pricing Strategy `/pricing-strategy`

Design pricing, tiers, and packaging for SaaS products.

### The three pricing axes
1. **Packaging**: What's included at each tier? How tiers differ.
2. **Pricing metric**: What do you charge for? (per user, per usage, flat fee)
3. **Price point**: The actual dollar amounts.

### Pricing models
| Model | Best for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Flat rate | Simple products, one ICP | Basecamp ($99/month) |
| Per seat | Team products where value scales with users | Slack, Notion |
| Usage-based | Infrastructure, API, data products | Stripe (% of transaction) |
| Tiered | Multiple ICPs with different needs | Most SaaS |
| Freemium | PLG motion, large market | Dropbox, Spotify |

### Freemium vs. free trial
- **Free trial**: Time-limited access to full product. Best for complex products.
- **Freemium**: Permanent free tier with feature limits. Best for network effects and PLG.

### What it asks
- Business and product type
- Target market (SMB, mid-market, enterprise)
- GTM motion (self-serve, sales-led, hybrid)
- Competitive pricing
- Current conversion rate, ARPU, and churn
- Goals: growth, revenue, or profitability

### Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter
Four questions to find acceptable price range:
1. At what price is this too cheap to trust?
2. At what price is this a bargain?
3. At what price is this getting expensive but still worth it?
4. At what price is this too expensive?

The acceptable range falls between "too cheap" and "too expensive."

## Competitor Alternatives `/competitor-alternatives`

Build comparison and alternative pages for SEO and sales enablement.

### Four page types
1. **Singular alternative**: "[Competitor] Alternative" — targets people considering one specific competitor
2. **Plural alternatives**: "Best [Competitor] Alternatives" — targets people actively switching
3. **You vs competitor**: "[Your Product] vs [Competitor]" — direct comparison
4. **Competitor vs competitor**: "[Competitor A] vs [Competitor B]" — captures research traffic

### What it asks
- Your product's core differentiators
- Your ideal customer profile
- Direct and indirect competitors
- Your honest strengths and weaknesses
- Search volume for competitor terms

### Page structure
- Honest comparison table (do not hide where you lose)
- Your unique differentiator section
- Who each product is best for
- Customer testimonials from switchers
- Clear CTA for your product

### Key principle
Be honest. Pages that try to spin every category in your favor lose credibility. Acknowledge where competitors are better, then explain who is a better fit for your product.
