## Overview
- **Author:** Sandra Cisneros (published 1984)
- **Narrator:** Esperanza Cordero — a young Latina girl growing up in Chicago
- **Format:** 44 vignettes (short, lyrical chapters — almost like prose poems)
- **Setting:** A Latino neighborhood in Chicago

---

## Major Themes

| Theme | What to Look For |
|---|---|
| **Identity & Belonging** | Esperanza's name, her house, how she sees herself vs. how others see her |
| **Home** | The house on Mango Street vs. the house she dreams of — what "home" means emotionally |
| **Gender & Confinement** | Women trapped by marriage, men, or circumstance (Rafaela, Sally, Mamacita) |
| **Language & Voice** | Writing as power — Esperanza becoming a storyteller as a form of escape |
| **Community** | Neighbors as a tapestry — each vignette adds a face to the street |
| **Dreams & Escape** | Esperanza's desire to leave vs. her responsibility to return |

## Key Characters

| Character | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Esperanza** | Narrator/protagonist | Wants to escape Mango Street but feels tied to it |
| **Nenny** | Esperanza's younger sister | Shares her laughter but not her dreams |
| **Marin** | Older girl on the street | Waiting to be rescued by a man — contrast to Esperanza |
| **Alicia** | College student | Represents education as escape |

| **Mamacita** | Neighbor | Refuses to speak English — language as identity |
| **The Three Sisters** | Mysterious aunts | Tell Esperanza she must "come back" |

## Key Vignettes — Quick Notes

### "The House on Mango Street"
- Esperanza is ashamed of her house — it is not the house she was promised
- Sets up the central tension: longing for a better home

### "My Name"
- Her name means "hope" in English but carries sadness in Spanish
- Named after her great-grandmother — a woman who never escaped
- Key quote opportunity: the tension between inheritance and self-determination

### "Boys & Girls"
- Boys and girls exist in separate worlds on Mango Street
- Esperanza wants a best friend who is "her other self"

### "Four Skinny Trees"
- Esperanza identifies with the trees — they survive despite not belonging
- Lyrical, metaphorical — good for close reading

### "Mango Says Goodbye Sometimes"
- Circular ending — returns to the opening
- Esperanza accepts she comes from Mango Street but won't be defined by it
- "I have gone away to come back. For the ones I left behind."

## Writing Tips for Journal Entries
- **Quote format:** "Quote here" (*Vignette Title*)
- Connect your quote directly to your argument — don't just drop it in
- Each paragraph = one idea, fully developed
- Paragraph 1: introduce your argument + first piece of evidence
- Paragraph 2: deepen the argument + second piece of evidence or counter-nuance
