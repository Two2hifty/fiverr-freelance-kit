# The Last Showing

*An original horror short story — scene-by-scene with image prompts*

---

## Scene 1 — The Empty Parking Lot

Marcus pulled into the Regal Cinema at 11:47 PM. The marquee was still lit — white bulbs buzzing against the cold — but the parking lot was wrong. Not empty the way a lot gets after everyone leaves. Empty the way a place gets when it decides it doesn't want people anymore. One shopping cart sat motionless in the middle of the asphalt, no wind to explain it.

He checked his phone. The listing said the midnight showing of *Hollow Ground* was still on. He was the only one here.

He got out of the car anyway.

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 1**
> A near-empty movie theater parking lot at midnight. A lone car parked under the glowing white marquee that reads "THE LAST SHOWING – MIDNIGHT". The lot is wet from recent rain, reflecting the sign's light in pale, ghostly streaks across the dark asphalt. A single shopping cart sits dead-center in the middle of the empty lot, perfectly still. No people. Fog creeping at the edges of the frame. Overhead sodium lights flicker. Cold, blue-white color palette. Photorealistic, cinematic, horror atmosphere, wide establishing shot.

## Scene 2 — The Lobby

The front doors opened on their own. Not with a hiss — slowly, like something had leaned on them from the inside. The lobby smelled of burnt popcorn and something underneath it, something older and wet, like a basement after a flood. The concession stand was lit and fully stocked. The popcorn machine tumbled kernels in slow, rhythmic turns.

No one was behind the counter.

A paper ticket sat on the glass — already torn, already used, with his name handwritten on it in red ink. *Marcus. Theater 7.*

He picked it up. The ink was still damp.

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 2**
> Inside a vintage movie theater lobby, ornate red-and-gold decor, polished marble floors. The concession stand is fully lit and stocked with candy and popcorn, the popcorn machine still spinning, but completely unmanned. No employees anywhere. On the glass counter sits a single paper ticket with "MARCUS – THEATER 7" handwritten in red ink that appears freshly written and slightly smeared. The lobby is otherwise perfectly still and silent. Warm amber light contrasts with a deep, creeping shadow toward the theater hallways. Photorealistic, unsettling, cinematic horror, eye-level shot.

## Scene 3 — The Hallway

Theater 7 was at the end of the corridor, past all the others. The hallway carpet — red with a gold diamond pattern — muffled his footsteps completely. He couldn't hear himself walk. The numbered doors on either side were all closed. All except one: Theater 4, cracked open just enough that he could see the screen inside was on, projecting something — not a film, just static, the white-gray hiss of a dead channel filling the dark room from floor to ceiling.

He kept walking. The carpet seemed to pull at his shoes.

He counted the doors. Theater 5. Theater 6. The hallway kept going. He counted again. Theater 6. Theater 6. Theater 6.

He stopped. Behind him, every door in the hall was now open.

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 3**
> A long, narrow movie theater hallway with dark red carpet and dim golden wall sconces. All the numbered theater doors (4, 5, 6) line both sides of the corridor. Every single door is hanging open, revealing pure darkness inside each theater — except Theater 4, which shows a screen blazing with white TV static. At the far end of the hallway stands a single closed door labeled "7" in tarnished brass numbers. The hallway stretches unnaturally long, creating a strong forced-perspective vanishing point. One figure — a teenage boy — stands mid-hallway, looking back over his shoulder at the open doors. Photorealistic, psychological horror, dim warm lighting, deep shadows, cinematic wide-angle.

## Scene 4 — Theater 7

The theater was full. Every seat taken, but no one moved. No rustling, no whispers, no phone screens. They sat perfectly upright — men, women, children — all facing the blank white screen, which had not yet started. The air in the room was cold enough that Marcus could see his breath.

He found the only empty seat, middle row, dead center.

He sat down.

The person to his left turned and looked at him. A woman in a yellow dress, 1970s style, her face pale as dry paper. She smiled. Her teeth were too many and too long.

"It's about to start," she said.

The lights went down.

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 4**
> Inside a packed movie theater, every seat filled with silent, motionless people of different ages and eras — some dressed in modern clothes, others in clothing from the 1950s, 60s, 70s — all facing a bright white blank movie screen. The theater is dark except for the glow of the screen, casting pale white light across dozens of still, expressionless faces. In the center of the middle row sits one modern teenage boy looking visibly out of place and terrified. Beside him, a woman in a yellow vintage dress turns toward him, her face too white, smiling with too many teeth. The contrast between the normal movie theater setting and the supernatural crowd creates deep dread. Photorealistic, cinematic horror, cold blue-white lighting from the screen, packed crowd shot from the back of the theater.

## Scene 5 — The Screen Turns On

The movie that played was not *Hollow Ground*. It was footage of the theater itself — this theater, Theater 7 — but empty. Filmed from above, a bird's-eye angle, showing all the seats vacant and dark. Then the footage zoomed in, slowly, on the center of the middle row. On one seat.

On Marcus.

He was already on the screen. Already sitting there. The timestamp in the corner of the footage read 11:47 PM — the same time he'd pulled into the parking lot. The film had already been made. He was already in it. He had been in it the whole time.

The woman in the yellow dress reached over and took his hand in hers. Her fingers were ice.

"Don't worry," she said, eyes fixed on the screen. "Everyone finds their seat eventually."

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 5**
> A large movie theater screen showing security-camera-style footage of the same theater — shot from a high corner angle, black and white, timestamp reading "11:47 PM" in the corner. The footage shows the theater completely empty except for one boy sitting alone in the center seat, looking up at the screen in horror, realizing he is watching himself. The real audience of motionless people fills the seats below, watching. A ghostly woman in a yellow dress sitting beside the boy reaches over and holds his hand, smiling calmly at the screen. Close-up shot of the screen within the theater, the footage-within-footage creating a recursive, trap-like feeling. Photorealistic, surreal horror, cold white screen light, deeply unsettling.

## Scene 6 — The Exit That Isn't

Marcus ran. Down the aisle, up the steps, shoved through the exit door at the back of the theater — and came out into the lobby. The same lobby. Popcorn machine still turning. The ticket still on the counter. He ran for the front doors. They opened slowly, leaning outward, and he burst through into the parking lot.

His car was gone. In its place, a shopping cart sat dead-center under the marquee. The sign above now read: *MARCUS — NO EXIT — ALL SEATS FILLED.*

He turned back. The lobby doors had already closed.

Behind the glass, in the dark, the woman in the yellow dress stood watching him, her hands folded, smiling her too-wide smile.

---

> [!note] **Nanobana Image Prompt — Scene 6**
> A teenage boy stands alone in a dark, rain-soaked movie theater parking lot at midnight, staring back at the theater's glass lobby doors in terror. Inside the lobby, barely visible through the glass, stands the silhouette of a woman in a yellow dress, motionless, watching him. The marquee above reads "MARCUS – NO EXIT – ALL SEATS FILLED" in glowing white bulbs. His car is gone. A lone shopping cart sits under the marquee lights. The lot is utterly empty. Rain begins to fall. The reflection of the marquee letters shimmers in the wet asphalt at his feet. Photorealistic, cinematic horror, cold and isolating, overhead shot slightly angled down, the theater looming large behind the small lone figure.

## Story Notes
- Style inspired by Joe Hill (*20th Century Ghosts*) — grounded setting, slow dread, supernatural twist
- Central technique: **pacing and delay** — each scene withholds one key piece of information
- Theme: being trapped in a story you can't escape (mirrors the assignment's focus on suspense techniques)
- All Nanobana prompts specify: photorealistic, cinematic, horror atmosphere, lighting source, shot type
