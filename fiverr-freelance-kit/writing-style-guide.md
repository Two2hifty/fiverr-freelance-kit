# WRITING STYLE GUIDE
### The rules that make every piece of content sound professional and consistent.

---

## WHY THIS GUIDE EXISTS

Clients hire you for your judgment, not just your typing. This guide gives you a repeatable standard so every blog post and email you deliver sounds intentional.

Follow these rules on every order, for every client.

---

## PART 1: CORE WRITING PRINCIPLES

### Write for the reader, not for yourself
- Every sentence should serve the reader. If it does not help them understand, act, or feel something, cut it.
- Ask: "So what?" after every claim. If you cannot answer it, rewrite the sentence.

### Use active voice
- Active: "The software tracks your conversions."
- Passive: "Your conversions are tracked by the software."
- Active voice is shorter and clearer. Default to it.

### Write short sentences
- Aim for 15 words or fewer per sentence.
- Long sentences lose readers. Break them up.

### Use short paragraphs
- Blog posts: 2 to 3 sentences per paragraph.
- Emails: 1 to 2 sentences per paragraph.
- White space keeps readers moving through your content.

### Be specific
- Weak: "This generates a lot of traffic."
- Strong: "This strategy drove 4,200 visits in 30 days."
- Numbers and specifics build trust. Vague claims do not.

---

## PART 2: TONE

Match the client's tone using the intake form answers. These are the four most common tones you will write in:

| Tone | Sounds Like | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Professional | Clear, precise, confident | Stiff, cold, jargon-heavy |
| Friendly/Conversational | Warm, direct, like a helpful colleague | Overly casual, sloppy |
| Bold/Direct | Short punchy sentences, no fluff | Aggressive, condescending |
| Educational | Step-by-step, well-organized, thorough | Boring, textbook-style |

### When in doubt, write friendly and direct.
It works across almost every niche.

---

## PART 3: BLOG POST RULES

### Structure every post like this:
1. Hook (first 2 sentences grab attention)
2. Problem (why the reader is here)
3. Solution (your main content)
4. CTA (one clear action to take)

### Headlines (H2/H3)
- Make every headline a benefit or a promise.
- Weak: "Introduction to Email Marketing"
- Strong: "How Email Marketing Earns $42 for Every $1 Spent"

### Introductions
- Never start with "In today's digital world..." or similar filler.
- Start with a stat, a bold statement, or a question.
- Get to the point within 3 sentences.

### Keyword placement
- Primary keyword: in the title, first 100 words, one H2, and the meta description.
- Secondary keywords: spread them naturally throughout the body.
- Never stuff keywords. If a sentence sounds forced, rewrite it.

### Meta descriptions
- Length: 150 to 160 characters.
- Include the primary keyword.
- Write a benefit or a reason to click. Do not summarize the article.

---

## PART 4: EMAIL RULES

### Subject lines
- Keep them under 50 characters.
- Curiosity and specificity outperform hype every time.
- Weak: "You won't believe this deal!!!"
- Strong: "3 things your welcome email is missing"

### Preview text
- Treat it as a second subject line.
- It should add context, not repeat the subject.
- Keep it between 40 and 90 characters.

### Email body
- Open with the reader's problem, not a self-introduction.
- One email = one message. Do not cover multiple topics.
- End every email with one CTA. Not two. One.

### CTAs (calls to action)
- Be direct: "Book your free call", "Download the guide", "Get 20% off today".
- Avoid weak CTAs like "Learn more" or "Click here".
- Put the CTA at the end of the email, after you have earned the click.

---

## PART 5: WORDS TO USE AND AVOID

### Use these words and phrases:
- You, your, you'll, you're (speak directly to the reader)
- Because (readers trust reasons)
- For example (specifics build credibility)
- Results, proof, data, numbers

### Avoid these words entirely:
- Just, very, really, basically, actually, certainly, probably
- Utilize (use "use")
- Leverage (use "use" or name the action)
- Game-changer, revolutionary, disruptive
- Unlock, discover, dive deep, delve
- In conclusion, to summarize, in closing

### Avoid these constructions:
- "Not only X, but also Y" (split into two sentences)
- Starting sentences with "It is important to note that..."
- Passive voice phrases: "It was found that...", "This can be seen in..."

---

## PART 6: FORMATTING RULES

### Use formatting to help readers scan
- Headers break up long sections.
- Bullet points replace dense paragraphs when listing 3 or more items.
- Bold key terms sparingly. If everything is bold, nothing is.

### Deliver in Google Docs
- Use Heading 2 for H2, Heading 3 for H3.
- Leave one blank line between paragraphs.
- Do not use tables unless the client asked for them.

---

## PART 7: SELF-EDIT CHECKLIST

Before you deliver any piece of content, run through this list:

- [ ] Did I read the intake form and match the client's tone?
- [ ] Does the first sentence hook the reader?
- [ ] Is every paragraph 3 sentences or fewer?
- [ ] Did I use active voice throughout?
- [ ] Is the primary keyword in the title, intro, and meta description?
- [ ] Is the CTA clear and direct?
- [ ] Did I remove all filler words (just, very, basically, etc.)?
- [ ] Did I rewrite any sentence that felt forced or robotic?
- [ ] Is the content free of spelling and grammar errors?
- [ ] Would I send this if my name were attached to it?

If you answer no to any of these, fix it before you deliver.

## QUICK REFERENCE

| Rule | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Active | Passive |
| Sentence length | Under 15 words | Run-on sentences |
| Paragraph length | 2 to 3 sentences | Walls of text |
| Specificity | Numbers and examples | Vague claims |
| CTA | One per email | Multiple competing CTAs |
| Tone | Match the intake form | Your personal writing style |
| Keywords | Natural placement | Keyword stuffing |
