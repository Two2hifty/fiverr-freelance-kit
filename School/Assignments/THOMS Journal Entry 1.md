## My Response

As can be seen in the opening scenes of *The House on Mango Street*, Esperanza does not try to hide how she really feels about where she lives. She knows that her parents promised her that one day she would have a real house with running water, real stairs, and a real yard. The house on Mango Street does not have any of these things. It is small, cramped, with narrow steps and windows so narrow that the house seems embarrassed to be seen. Esperanza seems to be reading off a list of everything that was supposed to be different about the house.

Esperanza's emotions about home are mostly about shame. When a nun from her school sees where she lives and looks at her with obvious shock, something changes in Esperanza's attitude. As can be seen from the quote, "I knew then I had to have a house. A real house. One I could point to" (Cisneros 5), it becomes obvious that home for Esperanza is not just about having a place where she can stay, it's about having a place where she can be proud of herself, where she does not feel less than others. Home for Esperanza seems to be about everything she wants but does not have yet.
